What your audience saves — vs. what they just talk about

Live TikTok & X conversation on functional smoothie ingredients, ranked two ways: reach (how big the conversation is) and buy-intent (how often people save the recipe — the truest signal of "I'll actually make this").

Source: Waldo discover · TikTok (sorted by likes, trailing year) + X Sample: 11 ingredient/benefit queries · ~19 top posts each For: The Daily Beet

Buzz and buy-intent are two different maps. The named adaptogens your audience talks about most — ashwagandha, sea moss, lion's mane — sit at the bottom: high awareness, almost nobody saves the recipe. What they actually save is framed as a benefit: "anti-inflammatory," "gut health," "hormone balance." Put the ingredient in the cup; sell the feeling on the menu.

Each ingredient plotted by total reach (likes, log scale) against save-intent (saves per 1,000 views). Up-and-right is the launch sweet spot: big conversation and high recipe intent. Bottom row is buzzword territory — loud, but nobody's making it.

02How they talk about it — the tone check

Reach doesn't tell you if the buzz is safe to build on. Reading the actual X conversation, two groups split cleanly on conviction.

Safe to lean on

warm
Ashwagandha — genuinely beloved as a supplement: "started taking it and immediately stopped being depressed" (32K likes). But the talk is about pills & dosing, not smoothies — borrow the halo, don't headline it.
high intent
Anti-inflammatory — the top post is hibiscus agua de jamaica: deep-red, botanical, recipe-native. On-brand and the #1 save-magnet.
high intent
Gut health — "debloat," "power house for your gut," framed as everyday and easy. Big and saved.

Handle with care

skeptical
Sea moss — the conversation is mostly questions: "what are the benefits?", "is it worth the hype?" Curiosity, not conviction. Low save intent.
caution
Ashwagandha (claims) — a real dosing-anxiety undertone: "takes your cortisol to zero," "people say it makes you numb." Naming a benefit on the menu invites the argument.
saturated
Collagen / lion's mane — steady awareness, thin recipe pull. Fine as an add-in, weak as a hook.
Rule for the menu copy: name the ingredient, describe the feeling ("calm," "glow," "debloat"), and skip the medical claim. The audience saves outcomes and argues with claims.

03The launch call

Where the intent, the tone, and The Daily Beet's crimson-and-farm-to-table identity all point at the same cup.

Lead SKU

A crimson anti-inflammatory smoothie

Highest save-intent lane (42/1K) and your namesake color — beet + hibiscus + ginger + a squeeze of citrus. The one benefit the monochrome-green category can't own visually, and the top-performing posts are already deep red.

Ride the reach

Pair the benefit with a protein or matcha base

Protein (1.5M) and matcha (1.45M) are the mainstream volume anchors. A "gut-glow" matcha or a protein-forward variant rides that reach while the benefit framing keeps it differentiated.

Secret ingredient

Moringa as the quiet flex

Tiny reach but 28/1K save-intent — a credible "you've-never-tried-this" add-in for the in-the-know regular. A story, not a headline.

Don't headline

Ashwagandha / sea moss on the marquee

Huge buzz, low recipe pull, and a claims-argument waiting to happen. Offer them as a +$1 booster for the crowd that already wants them — capture the search traffic without betting the launch on it.
